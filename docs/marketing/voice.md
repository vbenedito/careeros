## How this voice was built

There are no personal writing samples. The brand is a faceless account (D8), so the voice is taken from the writing CareerOS already has: the [landing content](../landing/content.md) (sections 4 and 5), the brand profile, and the persona's list of words to use and avoid. Confidence is **medium**: the rules are specific, but no social post exists yet. After the first 10 posts, review this page against what was really published, and fix it.

Every example below uses only true product facts (brand profile, section 6) or is labeled **sample**.

## 1. Voice in four traits

### Calm

- **Is:** steady, even, no pressure. It states what is true and stops.
- **Is not:** urgent, loud, guilt-driven, or full of "you must".
- **Example:** "Some weeks are small. A measured small week still tells you something."
- **Not this:** "Stop wasting time. Study harder."

### Clear

- **Is:** short sentences, common words, one idea at a time. A B2 reader understands it on the first read.
- **Is not:** clever, abstract, or full of jargon and buzzwords.
- **Example:** "It pauses after 60 seconds without keyboard or mouse input."
- **Not this:** "Smart idle detection keeps your data clean."

### Exact

- **Is:** a number, a name or a rule where other brands use an adjective. It says what is stored and what is not.
- **Is not:** vague, rounded up, or bigger than the truth.
- **Example:** "It stores the site name and the start and end times. Nothing else about your browsing, unless you turn on Track LeetCode problems."
- **Not this:** "Your privacy is our top priority."

### Respectful

- **Is:** "you" and "your", treats the reader as a skilled developer who reads permission prompts. Gives facts and lets them decide.
- **Is not:** pushy, shaming, or fake-friendly. It never compares you with other people, and it never sets an hour target.
- **Example:** "Pick your study sites once. Study as usual."
- **Not this:** "Don't fall behind. Others are putting in 12 hours a day."

One line to keep in mind: **we sound like a careful colleague with a clear chart, never like an ad.**

## 2. Tone by situation

The voice stays the same. The tone moves a little. Pick the row that matches the post.

| Situation | Tone | Length | What to do | What to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Educational post** (a tip about measuring study, a habit, how the product works) | Teacher, calm, practical | 3 to 6 short lines, or 5 to 7 slides | Start with the reader's question. Give one idea. End with one plain next step. | Lists of 10 tips. Claims about what "works" with no source. Hour targets. |
| **Product demo video text** | Neutral guide, step by step | 4 to 8 screens, 3 to 7 words each | Show the real flow: choose sites, study, see your week. Use sample data and label it. | Feature lists. Anything not shipped. Sound-only information. |
| **Reply to a comment** | Warm, short, direct | 1 to 3 sentences | Answer the question first. Link to the privacy page or listing when it helps. Say "We are not sure" before guessing. | Defending. Arguing. Naming or judging other tools. Promising a feature or a date. |
| **Bug or outage notice** | Plain, accountable, factual | 3 to 5 sentences | Say what happened, who is affected, what you should do, and when the next update comes. Say "we" and own it. | Blaming users or providers. Minimizing ("small issue"). Guessing the cause or a fix time. |
| **Celebrating a user milestone** (without real user data) | Quietly glad | 1 to 3 sentences | Speak to the reader's own milestone in general terms, or use a labeled sample. | Real user names, screenshots, counts or quotes. Praise that sounds like a promise of results. |

Notes for the two harder rows:

- **Bug or outage.** Template: "[What broke] since [time]. [Who is affected]. Your saved study time is [safe or not: only say it when it is confirmed]. We will post the next update by [time]." Never write a sentence about data safety that you have not checked.
- **Milestone.** CareerOS has no user numbers we can show (brand profile, section 6). So the post celebrates the reader's own habit, not a user's data. Example: "First full week measured? That is a real baseline. Next week you can plan from it." A milestone post may use a **sample** week, labeled as sample. It never uses a real user's data, and it never says "users have studied X hours".

## 3. Words

### Use

| Word or phrase | Use it for |

| study time | The thing CareerOS measures. |
| your real week | The promise. Pairs with "remembered week". |
| measured, measure | What the product does. |
| automatically, no timer | How it counts. True: there is no timer to start. |
| the sites you choose | The scope. "Up to 12 sites; subdomains included." |

| free | Price. Say only "free". |
| public code on GitHub | Code status. |
| free Chrome extension and web dashboard | What the product is. |
| delete your account anytime | Control. |

### Avoid

| Avoid | Why | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| revolutionary, game-changer, supercharge, 10x, unlock, next-level | Hype (brand profile, section 8). | A number or a plain fact. |
| grind, hustle, crush it, beast mode | Shame and pressure (persona). | "study", "practice", "steady". |
| hours-per-day targets, "you should study N hours" | Persona rule: no hour targets. | "Measure a normal week first." |
| "get hired", "land FAANG", "ace your interview", "get the offer" | No job-offer promises. | "Know how much you really studied." |
| stays free, always free, free forever | No "stays free" promise. | "free" |
| open source | There is no license. | "public code on GitHub" |
| no cookies, cookie-free | The dashboard uses session cookies for sign-in. | Say what is stored. |
| 100% private, totally secure, military-grade, zero tracking | Overstatement. | The exact list in "Privacy, exactly" below. |
| tracks your productivity, watches your screen, spy | Wrong picture of the product. | "measures study time on the sites you choose" |
| AI-powered, smart, intelligent | Not what the product is. | Describe the rule: "pauses after 60 seconds without input". |
| coach, course, question bank, tracker of solved problems | Not what it is (brand profile, section 3). | "It measures; it does not teach." |
| users love it, trusted by, rated, "studies show" | We have no such facts. | Nothing. Use no social proof. |
| anything about the LeetCode per-problem feature | Not shipped (brand profile, section 11). | Nothing, until it ships. |
| names of other trackers, "better than" | No attacking competitors. | Explain how CareerOS works. |
| emoji, exclamation marks | Brand profile, section 8. | A plain full stop. |

### Describing the product, exactly

Use one of these forms. They match the brand profile (sections 1, 2 and 6).

- **One line:** "CareerOS measures your interview-prep study time automatically, only on the sites you choose, and keeps everything else private."
- **Short:** "A free Chrome extension and web dashboard that counts your study time on the sites you choose."
- **Not this:** "the all-in-one prep platform", "your AI study companion", "productivity tracker".

### Privacy, exactly

Say this, and no more than this (brand profile, section 6; SEC-01):

- It counts only on sites you choose, up to 12.
- It stores only the site name and the start and end times. If you turn on Track LeetCode problems (off by default), it also stores the problem name from a LeetCode address, for example two-sum.
- It never stores full web addresses, page titles, page content or keystrokes.
- It never tracks incognito windows.
- It has no ad trackers.
- The code is public on GitHub.
- You can delete your account anytime. Data is removed right away. Encrypted backups expire within 90 days.

Rules for privacy lines:

- Use "never stores" for what is not stored. Do not use "never sees" or "never reads" alone. "Never reads page content" is fine. The extension reads the address of your active tab to find the site name. If a reader asks, say it: "It uses the tab's address only to get the site name."
- Do not claim "no cookies", "no data collected" or "fully anonymous". Your account email and sign-in provider are stored.
- Permissions, if you list them: `tabs`, `idle`, `alarms`, `storage`. No content scripts.

### Idle rules, exactly

- "Pauses after 60 seconds without keyboard or mouse input."
- "Keeps counting while the study tab plays sound, for up to 15 minutes without input."
- It measures active time on chosen sites. It does not measure focus or learning. Say so when asked.

## 4. Grammar and style

**Sentences**

- Aim for 8 to 15 words. Never more than 25.
- One idea per sentence. Cut "very", "really", "just", "actually".
- Use the active voice and simple verbs: "counts", "pauses", "stores", "shows".
- Use contractions in replies and captions ("it's", "you're"). Skip them in privacy lines, where the exact statement matters.
- Use "you" and "your". Use "we" for the team in notices and replies. Do not write "users" when "you" works.
- A B2 reader is the target. Avoid idioms and phrasal verbs that are hard to read ("figure out", "line up"). Prefer "find", "plan".

**Numbers and durations**

- Durations use the app format: `45m`, `1h 05m`, `6h 40m`. Always two digits for minutes after the hour. Never "1:05" or "1 hour 5 minutes" in a post about the app's numbers.
- Use digits for numbers: "60 seconds", "12 sites", "15 minutes". Write "one" only when it is not a measure ("one idea").
- Put a unit on every number. Say what it counts.
- Any example numbers are labeled **sample** on the screen or in the caption.
- No invented stats, percentages or "on average". Never round a product fact up.

**Names and capitalization**

- **CareerOS**: one word, capital C and capital OS. Not "Career OS", "careeros" or "CAREEROS" in text. The handle and URLs may be lowercase.
- Say "career O S" when read aloud, like macOS. If an AI voice is ever tested, spell it "career O S" in the script.
- Product names as the owners write them: LeetCode, NeetCode, GreatFrontEnd, GitHub, Chrome, Google.
- Sentence case for titles and slide headings: "See where your study time really goes". No Title Case, no ALL CAPS for emphasis.
- Site names in code style or plain lowercase: `leetcode.com`.

**Punctuation and symbols**

- No exclamation marks. No emoji. No ellipsis for drama.
- Avoid long dashes as a style tic. Use a full stop or a colon.
- Use one question at most per post, and only a real question the reader asks ("How much did you really study this week?").

**Hashtags**

- Use 3 to 5 topic hashtags only where the platform uses them. The platform study (#188) can change the count per platform.
- Put them at the end of the caption, never inside a sentence.
- Topic tags, not hype tags. Examples: `#codinginterview`, `#leetcode`, `#softwareengineer`, `#studyplan`, `#chromeextension`. Check each tag on the platform before use.
- No tags that promise results (`#gethired`, `#faang`).

**Links**

- Every social link carries `utm_*` parameters (D25). Use lowercase, no spaces, and the same names every time.
- Pattern: `https://careeros-taupe.vercel.app/?utm_source=<platform>&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=<short-topic>`
- Example: `https://careeros-taupe.vercel.app/?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=real-week`
- One link per post. Put it at the end, before the hashtags.
- Say what the link is: "Free Chrome extension and dashboard:" then the link.

## 5. On-screen text for text-only videos

Videos start as text on screen plus music, no voice (D13). They must work with the sound off, and they need to be readable on a small phone screen.

- **One idea per screen.** If a screen needs "and", split it.
- **3 to 7 words per line, 2 lines at most.** Break lines at natural phrases. Never leave one word alone on a line.
- **Show each screen long enough to read twice.** Rule of thumb: 1 second for every 2 words, plus 1 second. Do not rush.
- **Screen 1 is the hook.** A real question or a plain fact. No clickbait that the video does not pay off.
- **Order:** problem (1 to 2 screens), the idea (1), proof or demo (2 to 4), one next step (1). End on "Free Chrome extension" and the name, not on a hard sell.
- **Use sentence case.** Keep the same font, size and position on every screen.
- **High contrast.** Meet at least WCAG AA (brand profile, section 12). Do not use color alone to show meaning.
- **Safe area.** Keep text away from the edges and from the platform's buttons and caption area.
- **Sample data on screen.** Write "Sample data" in the corner on every screen that shows numbers.
- **No real user data.** No real email, browsing or dashboard screenshot from a real account.
- **Music is a bed, not the message.** Nothing important lives only in the music. Use royalty-free or licensed music. Use the platform's own "AI-generated" label when it offers one (brand profile, section 10). Add "Made with AI" to the caption only for an AI voice or AI images.
- **Captions and alt text.** The text on screen is the caption. Put the same message in the post caption, in plain words, for people who use screen readers.

Short example (sample, 6 screens):

1. How much did you study this week?
2. Not how much you think.
3. How much you counted.
4. CareerOS counts study time on the sites you choose.
5. No timer to start.
6. Free Chrome extension. CareerOS.

## 6. Before and after

All "after" lines use true product facts or labeled sample data.

**1. Generic hype to exact**

- Before: "Supercharge your interview prep with AI-powered tracking!"
- After: "Pick your study sites once. CareerOS counts the time while you study."
- Why: no hype word, no AI claim, no exclamation mark. It says what happens.

**2. Adjective to number**

- Before: "Smart idle detection keeps your stats accurate."
- After: "It pauses after 60 seconds without keyboard or mouse input."
- Why: a rule the reader can check.

**3. Privacy slogan to exact list**

- Before: "Your privacy is our top priority. 100% secure and anonymous."
- After: "It stores the site name and the start and end times. Not full web addresses, page titles, page content or keystrokes."
- Why: says exactly what is stored, with no overstatement.

**4. Shame to calm**

- Before: "Only 4 hours this week? Stop being lazy and grind harder."
- After: "Some weeks are small. Measure this one, then plan the next from what really happened."
- Why: no shame, no hour target, no number the product does not have.

**5. Result promise to honest outcome**

- Before: "Track your hours and land your dream FAANG offer."
- After: "Know how much you really studied this week, and on which sites."
- Why: CareerOS measures time. It cannot promise an interview result.

**6. Fake proof to labeled sample**

- Before: "Join thousands of engineers who doubled their study time."
- After: "Sample week: 6h 40m. Your own week will look different." (Label the screen "Sample data".)
- Why: no user count, no result claim. The sample is labeled. The 6h 40m figure is the sample week used on the landing page.

## 7. Checklist before you publish

Run it on every post. If one answer is "no", fix the post first.

1. Does every fact match the brand profile, section 6? Can I point to the source?
2. Is every number either a product fact or labeled **sample**? No user counts, ratings, quotes or "studies show"?
3. Are privacy lines exact, with no "100%", "no cookies" or "open source"?
4. No job-offer promise, no hour target, no shame, no "grind"?
5. No future feature, date or price? "Free" only, never "stays free"? Nothing about the LeetCode per-problem feature until it ships?
6. No other tool named or mocked? No real user data, emails or browsing in text or images?
7. Short sentences, "you", no hype words, no exclamation mark, no emoji?
8. Durations in the app format (`45m`, `1h 05m`)? "CareerOS" spelled correctly?
9. 3 to 5 topic hashtags (if the platform uses them), and one link with `utm_*` parameters?
10. Video: one idea per screen, readable with the sound off, "Sample data" shown, captions and alt text added?
11. AI voice, AI images or AI music: platform "AI-generated" label on when offered? "Made with AI" in the caption only for an AI voice or AI images (brand profile, section 10)?
12. Read it aloud once. Would a calm colleague say this? If it sounds like an ad, rewrite it.
