## 1. The four pillars at a glance

A pillar is a theme CareerOS owns. It is not a format ("demo") and not an intent ("educational"). Each pillar below comes from one belief in the brand profile (section 7) and one pain in the persona.

| # | Pillar | Share | Belief it comes from | Signature? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Your real week | 40% | 1. You can only steer what you measure. 4. Plain numbers. | Yes |
| 2 | Steady, not heroic | 30% | 2. Steady beats heroic. | No |
| 3 | Exactly what is stored | 20% | 3. Privacy is not a feature to trade. | No |
| 4 | CareerOS at work | 10% | The product itself. | No (the only product-led pillar) |

Why these four. The platform study found white space for "measure your real study week, and say exactly what is stored" (platform study, section 4; small sample, assumption). Pillars 1 and 3 own that space. Pillar 2 serves the pains the persona names most: burnout, guilt and fading effort. Pillar 4 exists so product content has a small, clear place.

## 2. Pillar 1: Your real week (signature)

**Purpose.** Show that a measured week and a remembered week are different, and that a plain number is enough to plan the next week.

**Serves (persona).**
- Job: "See my real study time per day and per site" and "Stop guessing" (Jobs to be done).
- Pain: "Not knowing where the time goes" (Pains and fears).
- Trigger: "A bad week" and "A plan review, for example every Sunday" (Triggers).
- Words: "your real week", "measured", "per day, per site" (voice, section 3).

**May use.**
- Brand profile section 6 facts: counts on chosen sites (up to 12, subdomains included), no timer, pauses after 60 seconds without input, per day and per site views, works on more than one computer.
- A labeled sample week (6h 40m, from the voice page) drawn as plain bars or text, with "Sample data" on every screen that shows numbers. These are not app screenshots (see the demo rule in section 6).
- Our own belief, said as a belief: "We think a measured week beats a remembered week."

**Must never.**
- Say a remembered week is bigger "on average" or give any percentage. It is our premise, not a measured fact (persona, Current workarounds).
- Set an hour target or say how many hours a person "should" study.
- Show real user data, or a number without a unit or a "Sample data" label.
- Promise a result from measuring.
- Suggest that CareerOS has a plan feature. It does not. Plans live in the reader's own notes.

**Success signal (for #190 and #191).** Watch time or retention on each video, and shares. Read it in each platform's own analytics. Set thresholds in #191.

**Post ideas (20 to 45 s, text on screen).** LI = also suits a LinkedIn caption. Format tags: question, chart, list, steps, myth-vs-fact.

1. Question. "How much did you study this week?" Not how much you think. How much you counted. (LI)
2. Chart. Same sample week, two views drawn as plain bars: per day, then per site. (sample data)
3. Chart. "A week has seven days. Which two did the work?" One plain day-bar row. (sample data)
4. List. Many sites, one total: three study sites written as text lines, then the sum. (sample data)
5. Question. "Forgot the timer again?" Why a measure with no timer to start is easier to keep. (LI)
6. Steps. Plan in numbers in your own notes, then check the week on Sunday. This is a short view. CareerOS has no plan feature. (LI)
7. Chart. "Which site took the most time?" One plain bar per site, read calmly. (sample data)
8. List. Two computers, one week: the dashboard adds them up. Three text screens, no UI. (sample data)
9. Myth-vs-fact. "I know how my week went." Our view: memory guesses, a count does not. (LI)

## 3. Pillar 2: Steady, not heroic

**Purpose.** Replace guilt and comparison with a calm habit: several normal days beat one marathon day.

**Words on screen.** The pillar name is the brand point of view. On screen, use simple words. "Heroic" means one very long study day that is hard to repeat. Say "one very long study day" instead of "heroic", and "starting point" instead of "baseline".

**Serves (persona).**
- Pains: "Overload and fading effort", "Guilt", "Comparing with others".
- Job: "Replace guilt with a number I can trust" (Emotional jobs).
- Triggers: "Burnout or a long gap" and "A bad week".
- Words: "consistency", "steady" (use). "Grind" (avoid).

**May use.**
- Brand profile section 7 as opinion: "Steady beats heroic" and "More hours alone do not guarantee an offer". Label it as our view.
- A labeled sample week, drawn as plain text or bars.
- The persona's ideas in our own words. Do not use their phrases as unattributed quotes (persona, Their own words).

**Must never.**
- Shame or pressure: no "you are behind", no "grind harder", no hour targets.
- Compare the reader with other people, or invent what "others" study.
- Claim that steady study "works" or "is proven". No "studies show". Say "we think".
- Give medical or mental-health advice. We talk about habits and numbers only.
- Promise an interview result.
- Suggest that CareerOS compares weeks or has a plan feature. Comparing is the reader's own habit.

**Success signal (for #190 and #191).** Shares and saves, and retention. On LinkedIn, comments and clicks on the UTM link.

**Post ideas (20 to 45 s).**

1. Question. "Some weeks are small." A small measured week still tells you something. (LI)
2. Myth-vs-fact. Five ordinary days or one very long study day? Our view, said as a view. (LI)
3. Steps. After a missed day: look at the week, not the day. Three calm steps. (LI)
4. Steps. Restarting after a gap: measure one ordinary week first. It is a starting point, not a score. (LI)
5. List. Do not compare your week with someone else's. You can compare it with your own last week in your own notes. CareerOS does not do this for you. (LI)
6. Question. A guilt-free Sunday review in three questions: what did I plan, what did I count, what changes? Short view; CareerOS has no plan feature. (LI)
7. Question. Work is demanding every week. Plan the week you really have, not the week you wish for. (own words; the idea comes from a source in the persona)
8. List. Why we do not give an hours-per-day target, and what to use instead. (LI)
9. List. Evenings are short. Measure the evenings you really have, then plan from them. (LI)
10. Myth-vs-fact. "We think heroic hours are hard to keep." One very long study day, then a pause. Our view only. (LI)

## 4. Pillar 3: Exactly what is stored

**Purpose.** Win trust from careful developers by saying exactly what CareerOS stores about your study time, and no more.

**Hook rule.** Open with the reader's doubt, not with the list. Examples: "Does a study tracker watch everything you do?" or "That permission warning looks big. What does it do?" The pain is "tools that ask too much" (internal research only; never name or hint at another tool).

**Limit.** Fact-based ideas run out after about 8 weeks. Expect repeats then, with new hooks (voice: do not repost the same video). Decide at the 8-week review whether to keep, merge or drop this pillar.

**Serves (persona).**
- Objections: "Privacy: will it watch everything I do?", "The tab permission", "It will count when I am not really studying", "Can I leave?" (Objections table).
- Pain: "Tools that ask too much".
- Trigger: "Seeing a timer fail" and the careful read of a store page (assumption 4).

**May use.** Only brand profile section 6 and the voice page "Privacy, exactly" and "Idle rules, exactly":
- Counts only on sites you choose, up to 12.
- About your study time, it stores only the site name and the start and end times. If you turn on Track coding problems (off by default), it also stores the problem name from a leetcode.com or neetcode.io address, for example two-sum. (Your account email and sign-in provider are stored too. Say so if asked.)
- Never stores full web addresses, page titles, page content or keystrokes.
- Never tracks incognito windows. No ad trackers.
- Permissions: `tabs`, `idle`, `alarms`, `storage`. It does not read page content (SEC-01, SEC-03).
- Public code on GitHub (not "open source").
- Delete your account anytime; data removed right away; encrypted backups expire within 90 days.
- It measures active time on chosen sites, not focus or learning.

**Must never.**
- Say "100% private", "no cookies", "no data collected", "fully anonymous", "open source", "never sees".
- Name or mock another tool, or quote its reviews.
- Show real browsing, emails or a real account.
- Promise other sites or problem features (solved problems, difficulty, topics). Only time per problem on LeetCode or NeetCode is live (brand profile section 11).
- Answer a question we cannot check. Say "We are not sure" first (voice, section 2).

**Success signal (for #190 and #191).** Saves, and clicks on the profile link and the UTM link. A privacy post that gets saves but no clicks still builds trust; judge it at the review.

**Post ideas (20 to 45 s).**

1. List. "Does a study tracker watch everything you do?" Exactly what CareerOS stores about your study time. (LI)
2. List. "What it never stores": full web addresses, page titles, page content, keystrokes. About your study time, nothing more is stored. (LI)
3. Steps. "That permission warning looks big." Four permissions and why each is there: `tabs`, `idle`, `alarms`, `storage`. (LI)
4. Myth-vs-fact. "Can it read my pages?" It does not read page content. In plain words, what that means. (LI)
5. Question. "What happens to a site you did not choose?" It is ignored. (LI)
6. Myth-vs-fact. "Incognito windows are never tracked." One fact, short screens.
7. Question. "When does it stop counting?" 60 seconds without input; the 15-minute sound rule. (LI)
8. Myth-vs-fact. "Does it measure focus?" No. It measures time. What the number does and does not say. (LI)
9. Steps. "Can I leave?" Delete your account; what happens to the data and the 90-day backups. (LI)
10. List. "Do not trust, check." The code is public on GitHub; where to look. (LI)

## 5. Pillar 4: CareerOS at work (product-led, minority)

**Purpose.** Show the real product flow for a reader who is already interested. This is the only pillar that sells.

**Serves (persona).** Job: "Functional: see my real study time without starting a timer". Objection: "Another tool to remember". Trigger: "Starting a new prep cycle".

**May use.** Brand profile sections 2 and 6, sample data, and the free Chrome Web Store listing. Say only "free". Use the UTM link pattern from the voice page.

**Must never.**
- Show a feature that is not shipped (brand profile section 11), or one that does not exist: no week-over-week comparison, no plan feature.
- Use user counts, ratings, reviews or "stays free".
- Use a real account screenshot.
- Be the end of every post (see the product rule in section 6).

**Success signal (for #190 and #191).** Clicks on the UTM link and visits to the site or the store listing per hour spent. On LinkedIn, link clicks in the test weeks.

**Post ideas (20 to 45 s).** All are demos (section 6).

1. Steps. Setup in four screens: choose your study sites once; study as usual; open the dashboard; see the week. (sample data)
2. Steps. "Add a study site." Choose up to 12; subdomains are included.
3. Steps. A tour of the dashboard: day view and site view. (sample data)
4. Question. "No timer to start": what happens in the first minute of a study session.
5. Steps. Idle rule in action: the count pauses after 60 seconds without input. (sample data)
6. Steps. Sound rule: the count continues while a study tab plays sound, up to 15 minutes without input.
7. List. "Working offline": it uploads later. Only say what SYNC-01..08 states.
8. Steps. Sign in with Google or GitHub; what the dashboard shows after the first day. (sample data)
9. Steps. Week and month views: see each on its own chart. (sample data)
10. Steps. "Your first week with CareerOS": a calm walk-through from install to first chart. (sample data)

## 6. The mix and how often the product appears

Use the mix over each 8-week review period. At 2 videos a week (platform study, section 6) that is about 16 videos.

| Pillar | Share | About 16 videos |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Your real week | 40% | 6 to 7 |
| 2. Steady, not heroic | 30% | 5 |
| 3. Exactly what is stored | 20% | 3 |
| 4. CareerOS at work | 10% | 1 to 2 |

Product rules.
- **Definition.** A "demo" is any video that shows the extension or the dashboard UI (a screen recording or a screenshot of the product). It counts toward the product limit, whatever its pillar.
- **At most 1 in 10 posts is a demo.** Never two demos in a row. *(Amended 2026-11-25, product owner decision P10, spec 04: in the launch phase, weeks 1–8, one of the two weekly videos is an explainer that shows the real product UI with sample data (Tuesday), and the other stays in pillars 1–3 (Thursday). Demos may follow each other across weeks. Every video opens with a clear "what this is / who it is for" line in the first 3 seconds when the topic allows. From week 9 the 1-in-10 rule applies again unless the 8-week review changes it.)*
- Charts in pillar 1 are plain drawn bars or text lines, labeled "Sample data". They are not app screenshots, so they are not demos.
- Pillars 1 to 3 may end with one plain line and a link ("Free Chrome extension and dashboard:"). They do not show a product tour.
- Pillar 3 states product facts by nature. That is allowed, because the facts answer a doubt. It is a demo only if it shows the UI.
- No post in pillar 2 mentions a feature. It may end with the name only.
- This mix is a starting point (assumption). Change it only at the 8-week review, with the platform numbers (platform study, section 8). Never publish those numbers as statistics unless they are real.

## 8. Not now

- **Time per coding problem.** Live since extension 0.3.1 (brand profile section 11; D11, D16). Pillars 1, 3 and 4 may mention it (LeetCode or NeetCode, Track coding problems, off by default), with the exact privacy line in section 4. Pillar 2 does not need it.
- **Solved problems, difficulty, topics.** Not a CareerOS feature today (brand profile section 3).
- **Interview tips, question walk-throughs, "how to pass" content.** CareerOS measures; it does not teach (brand profile section 3). It would also drift toward job-offer promises.
- **User stories, testimonials, reviews, user counts, "hours saved".** We have none we can show (brand profile section 6).
- **Study-hour benchmarks or comparisons with "other engineers".** No data, and it breaks the no-comparison rule.
- **Naming or mocking other trackers, or reacting to their reviews.** Brand profile section 10.
- **Price, paid plan or roadmap teasers.** Brand profile section 10 (D5, D23).
- **A face, a founder story or "build in public" posts.** The account is faceless (D8).
- **Trend and meme formats, duets and stitches.** Not in the first 8 weeks; fewer posts at good quality (D14).
- **An AI voice.** A test for later (D13). If it is tested, add "Made with AI" in the caption (brand profile section 10).
- **A fifth pillar.** Wait until the 8-week review.
